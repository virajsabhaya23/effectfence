# Adoption and impact evidence

EffectFence can support an original-contribution record only through truthful,
independently corroborated use and impact. Repository existence, download
counts, and stars alone do not establish field-level significance.

Maintain release-by-release evidence in public issues or a versioned adoption
log. Useful evidence includes:

- independently authored integrations and reproducible reports;
- downstream products or CI pipelines that pin EffectFence;
- concrete defects found before production, with confidential details removed;
- external citations in papers, standards discussions, engineering articles,
  or conference material;
- maintainer or company statements explaining why the verifier mattered;
- accepted upstream changes motivated by an EffectFence counterexample.

For each claim, retain the public URL, date, project/release version, contact or
author, and a short explanation of the technical consequence. Separate verified
adoption from leads, private evaluations, and self-authored demonstrations. Do
not inflate metrics, solicit scripted testimonials, or describe a local fixture
as external adoption.

The most credible near-term milestone is three independent MCP server teams
running the same versioned conformance schema and publishing either a passing
report or a defect fixed from a failing report. That outcome would also expose
which observers and contract rules need standardization next.
